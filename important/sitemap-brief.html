Creditpaper Site Map

Twenty-three screens that take an Australian commercial-property lender from sign-up to a signed-off credit paper. The structure and the job of every page. Layout and visual identity are yours — a clickable walkthrough of the same journey is a separate document.

Zones 4 Screens 23 Roles analyst · credit officer · admin Status scoping in progress

What the product does

A lender drops a folder of deal documents — valuations, term sheets, quantity-surveyor reports, building contracts — and gets back a credit-committee paper written in their own house voice, where every figure traces to the page and clause it came from. A human writes the recommendation. Always.

01

The wow lands in ninety seconds

New tenants see one section of a credit paper in their own house voice — learned from their website and two or three of their signed papers — before they are ever asked for a card. Onboarding is a demo, not a form.

02

A run takes about sixteen minutes

Generating a paper is a long job, not a request. Progress belongs on the deal and in a global indicator. Never build a screen a user has to sit on and watch.

03

Nothing is ever silently dropped

Unrecognised documents, held checks, contradictory figures — all of it surfaces. This is a regulated document. A quiet omission is the worst failure the product can have.

04

The machine proposes, the human confirms

Document mapping, house style, findings — all are proposals until a person affirms them. Design for review and correction, not for acceptance.

05

Institutional register, not SaaS cheer

The product's own writing rule is "findings, not enthusiasm". No exclamation marks, no confetti, no celebratory empty states. Confident and quiet.

06

A gap is written, not reported

Where information is missing, the product does not print "not available". It writes the structure that section should have — real headings, the shape a strong paper would take — with each unknown left as a labelled blank and a note on where the answer usually comes from. The analyst accepts it inline and the section drops in fully scaffolded, marked incomplete until the document arrives. This behaviour shapes the review screen more than any other.

07

Delivered means final

Sections arrive one at a time as they are written, so a reviewer can start on section two while section six is still drafting. That only works if nothing shown to a person is ever changed by the machine afterwards — so a section appears only once it has been drafted, checked and proofread. The one thing that can still move is the facts underneath, when a new document arrives; then the reviewer is told precisely what changed and chooses when to take it.

Public

Five screens, all of them fast. The firm's identity comes from the email domain, so sign-up asks for almost nothing.

/signupCreate an account
Job

Get a real person to a verified account in under thirty seconds.

Contains
  • Microsoft, Google, or email and password — Microsoft first
  • Nothing else — no firm name, no role, no phone number
  • Link to sign in
States

Default · submitting · email already registered · firm already has an account (see note) · weak password

Note

Microsoft is the primary button — a boutique Australian lender is on Microsoft 365, so a work account brings their existing multi-factor policy, their conditional access and a verified domain with it, for no build. Restrict both providers to work and school accounts: a personal outlook.com or gmail.com is free-mail and must never identify a firm. The email domain identifies the firm. If someone signs up at a domain that already has an account, they do not get a second one — they see "Acme Capital already has an account" and can request access, which an admin approves. Consumer email addresses always get their own account.

/loginSign in
Job

Return a known user to wherever they were going.

Contains
  • Microsoft and Google buttons, then email and password
  • Forgot password link
  • Link to create an account
States

Default · submitting · wrong credentials · account not yet verified · access request still pending approval

Note

If they arrived from a deep link, send them there after sign-in rather than to the dashboard.

/forgotReset your password
Job

Send a reset link without revealing whether an address exists.

Email field, submit, and a confirmation that reads the same whether or not the address was found.

States

Default · submitting · sent · rate-limited

/resetChoose a new password
Job

Set a new password from an emailed one-time link.

Contains

New password, confirmation, strength indication, and a line stating that all other sessions will be signed out.

States

Valid token · expired token · already used · success

/verifyConfirm your email
Job

Hold the user until their address is confirmed.

Contains

Clear statement that a link was sent, the address it went to, a resend control with a cooldown, and a way to correct a mistyped address.

States

Awaiting · resent · verified (auto-advances to onboarding) · link expired

Note

This is a real gate, not a nag: nothing downstream runs until it passes. Design it as a calm waiting room, not an error.

Onboarding

Five steps, resumable, each remembering where the user got to. The whole sequence is built to reach step three fast — that is where the product proves itself.

/onboarding/brandYour brand
Job

Read the firm's website and show the product already wearing their identity. First small win, about ten seconds in.

Contains
  • The firm's domain, prefilled from their email — they should not have to type it
  • A live result: logo, colour palette, typefaces, and the words this firm uses for things
  • Confirm or correct, then continue
States

Scraping · found · thin result (site is sparse) · site unreachable · consumer email, so ask for the URL

Note

The website supplies look and vocabulary only. It must never influence how the paper is written — marketing language has no place in a credit document, and this boundary is enforced in the system, not by convention. Worth showing the user what was taken and what was not.

/onboarding/papersTeach it your voice
Job

Collect two or three of the firm's own signed credit papers, from which the product learns their structure and voice.

Contains
  • Drop zone for two or three documents
  • A plain statement of why: these are papers their credit committee already approved, so they are the standard
  • Assurance that they stay private to this firm and can be deleted at any time
  • A skip path — a new firm may not have any
States

Empty · uploading · learning · learned · skipped (falls back to a general style) · unreadable file

Note

This step must never dead-end. A firm with no prior papers still reaches the next screen and still sees the wow.

/onboarding/previewIn your voicethe wow
Job

The most important screen in the product. Show one section of a real credit paper written in this firm's voice, beside the generic version, so the difference is unmissable.

Contains
  • Side-by-side comparison: generic draft against theirs
  • Enough of the section to actually read — this is not a thumbnail
  • A short note on what was learned: their structure, their register, their terms
  • One forward action
States

Generating (about ninety seconds, and it should feel purposeful) · ready · fell back to general style

Note

The waiting state matters as much as the result. Show what is happening — reading your papers, learning your structure, drafting — because the wait is the demonstration. Written on a sample deal, so nothing of theirs is needed yet.

/onboarding/profileConfirm your house style
Job

Let the firm read back what was learned and correct it before it is used.

Contains
  • Voice and register, in plain words
  • Section order and headings
  • Their terminology — what they call a facility, a sponsor, a security
  • Every part editable
States

Learned · edited · fallback profile (nothing was uploaded)

Note

Same page as /settings/profile later, so build it once. Nothing is this firm's house style until they say it is.

/onboarding/planAdd a payment method
Job

Take a card, immediately after the product has proved itself.

Contains
  • What is included and what a paper costs
  • Three tiers banded on papers per month — 3, 8, or unlimited by quote
  • Embedded card fields — never a redirect to a hosted page
  • "Your first paper is on us. Nothing is charged today." — the card is stored now, and the plan starts once the first paper is approved
States

Default · processing · card declined · saved

Note

Deliberately placed after the wow. The user has already seen a paper in their own voice, so this reads as the next step rather than a toll gate. Bouncing them to another domain here would break the moment. The card is taken now but not charged: the friction of entering it belongs here, while intent is highest, not later when they are mid-deal and want a paper.

Workspace

Where the work happens. The deal is the unit — everything meaningful hangs beneath one.

/Deals
Job

The landing page. Show every deal and its state, and make starting a new one obvious.

Contains
  • A grid of deal cards, most recently touched first
  • New deal as the first tile, always
  • Per card: name, deal type, stage, last activity, who owns it
  • An attention marker when something is stuck — a held check, a figure that changed, an approval withdrawn
  • Search and filter
  • A menu on each tile — open, rename, export, archive. Delete appears only on deals that never reached approval
  • Progress labelled in words — "5 of 9 sections approved", "4 of 7 documents extracted"
Progress

Bars measure the current stage only, never the whole journey — a lifecycle percentage would need invented weightings. Every stage has an exact count available, so nothing is estimated, and the count is always written out beside the bar. If the denominator is not yet known, there is no bar. It measures work items, not time: six of nine sections approved is not two thirds of the remaining effort, because review is the slow part.

Deleting

An approved paper is a record and must be archived, not deleted — financial-services retention runs to years, and an approved paper carries an audit trail a regulator may later ask for. Archive hides it from the grid and is fully recoverable, which also keeps the active count meaningful. Every tile action is written to the deal's activity trail.

States

Empty (first ever visit) · populated · filtered to nothing · generating in the background · billing blocked

Note

A project dashboard, not an analytics one — think of a design tool's home screen rather than a reporting suite. No charts. Its job is to show what is stuck, not only what exists. The empty state is a conversion moment: one clear invitation to create the first deal.

/deals/newNew deal
Job

Get from intent to dragging documents in as few steps as possible.

Contains

Deal name and deal type — construction, bridge, or residual stock. Everything else optional and inline.

States

Default · submitting · duplicate name

Note

On save, land directly on the drop zone, not on an empty overview. The first thing anyone does in a new deal is add documents; do not make them find that.

/deals/:dealIdDeal overview
Job

Answer "where is this deal up to, and what needs me?"

Contains
  • Stage, and what moves it forward
  • Any job currently running, with progress and elapsed time
  • Outstanding documents — what is still missing
  • Anything held or contradictory, as work to do rather than a log entry
  • Tabs into documents, facts, paper, activity
States

Fresh · extracting · drafting · needs attention · in review · approved

/deals/:dealId/documentsDocuments
Job

Take a folder of forty to a hundred and eighty unsorted files and end with every one accounted for.

Contains
  • One drop zone. The user never picks a category before uploading
  • A board of every document type this deal needs — filled, processing, checked, flagged, or empty
  • For each proposed match: the filename and why this file was chosen over the others
  • Confirm or correct, one at a time — no confirm-all
  • An Unassigned tray for anything unrecognised
  • Superseded versions, still reachable
  • A failed extraction shown with a retry, and repeated in the deal's needs-you list
Removing

Three cases, and the difference matters. A document nothing has read yet is simply removed. Once facts cite it, removal is a withdrawal: the facts that came from it are invalidated and surfaced, never silently dropped. If an approved section cites it, withdrawal also revokes approval on that section. The document itself stays in the record as withdrawn — an approved paper that once cited it is an audit artefact.

States

Empty · uploading · proposals awaiting confirmation · all confirmed · unassigned files remain · a document was replaced by a newer one

Note

The hardest and most important screen in the product. Order the review by doubt — ambiguous and competing matches first, obvious ones below — so attention goes where it is needed. Empty slots are not a gap in the UI; they are the outstanding-items list that ends up disclosed in the paper. Better documents arrive weeks later and supersede earlier ones, so nothing here is ever final.

/deals/:dealId/factsFacts
Job

Show everything read out of the documents, and let any figure be traced to its source.

Contains
  • Facts grouped the way a credit person thinks — the facility, the security, the borrower, the valuation, the build
  • Every value showing its source document, page, and the exact wording it came from
  • Status on each: read, confirmed by a second document, flagged, or missing
  • Contradictions shown as pairs, with both figures visible
States

Empty · extracting · complete · contradictions outstanding

Note

This screen is the product's honesty made visible — every number in the paper can be traced back to a clause on a page. Click-through to the source should feel immediate. Numbers align in columns; use tabular figures.

/deals/:dealId/paperThe paper
Job

Start a draft, then show every section and where it stands.

Contains
  • Generate, with an honest estimate of time and cost before committing
  • Every section listed with its state: draft, edited, evidence incomplete, approved
  • Comment and edit counts per section
  • Export, and a free re-render after edits
  • A checkpoint after the overall read, about a minute in — every section inherits it, so a wrong read is worth catching before nine sections are written against it. It continues on its own if nobody is there
  • Issue history, and a change summary between any two issues
Issuing

A paper is issued as often as it needs to be — sometimes it is final at credit committee and sometimes it is not, so there is no committee stage to move through. Each issue is a fixed snapshot that can be compared with any other. The change summary leads with figures that moved, then material prose, then editorial, because a committee member cares far more that a cost changed than that a sentence was rephrased. It is generated without a model call and renders into the paper itself, beside the approval page.

Waiting

The first section arrives in about four minutes and the rest follow. Never build a screen someone must sit and watch — progress follows them, and a queued run says so with its position rather than appearing to hang.

States

Not yet generated · generating · drafted · partly approved · fully approved · a section failed · blocked on billing

Note

Generation takes roughly sixteen minutes. The user must be free to leave — progress follows them. A failed section does not lose the run; show which one and why, and let it be retried on its own.

/deals/:dealId/paper/:sectionSection review
Job

Read a section, mark it up, and approve it. This is where a credit professional spends their time.

Contains
  • The section as a proper document, not a text box
  • Tracked changes and suggestion mode — every edit attributed, reviewable, and reversible
  • Comments anchored to selected text
  • Cited figures traceable to source without leaving the page
  • Scaffolded gaps — where information is missing, the structure the section should have, with accept or reject inline
  • Points the system flagged for a human — accept or reject, never auto-corrected
  • Who else is in this section right now, and a lock while someone is editing
  • A figure slot — the engine proposes an image from a document the user already provided ("stamped site plan, DA pack page 3"), captioned with its source
  • Section navigation, and approve
  • Clear marking of what was machine-written and what a person changed
States

Draft · edited · open suggestions awaiting review · open comments · evidence incomplete · locked — someone else is editing · approved · approval withdrawn because a figure changed

Note

The busiest screen in the product, and the one to prototype first. Three things to get right:

Scaffolds

Accept and reject need to be fast — an analyst will move through several in a section. On accept, the structure drops into the prose and the section is marked incomplete until the missing document arrives. Link each accepted scaffold to the document slot that would fill it, so the gap becomes something to act on rather than a label. That closes the loop: gap accepted → outstanding item → document uploaded → section completes.

Editing

Redlining, suggestions and comments come from the editor library already chosen for this product, so follow its conventions rather than inventing new ones. Every edit is attributed and kept — the record of who changed what is a regulatory artefact, not a convenience.

Provenance

Clicking any cited figure opens its chain: the value, where it came from, and — for a calculated number — the inputs it was derived from, each clickable in turn. Every walk ends at a clause in a document, quoted, with a link to that page. That is not a promise about the interface, it is a property of the system: a figure cannot exist in the paper without a source. Worth saying out loud in the product, because it is the strongest trust claim available and it is provably true.

Reasoning

On request, never in the prose — the paper must read as a credit paper, not as annotated machine output. Two things are available: what this passage cites, and what shaped it — the overall read of the deal that every section inherits, and which of the firm's own rules were in force. Fuller rationale arrives later with the engine's typed findings; do not promise it before then.

Facts moving

A new document can land while someone is reading. Nothing changes under them — they stay on the version they opened and are told precisely what moved: "construction cost $4,703,611 → $4,850,000, from a revised QS report, in paragraph 3." Naming the change is what stops it feeling like unbounded rework. They keep reading, editing and commenting; only approval waits, because approving is the regulated act.

Together

Several people work a paper at once, but one section at a time each. Show who is where; lock a section while it is being edited rather than merging two people's keystrokes. For a document that has to answer "who wrote this sentence" with one name, an orderly queue beats live co-editing.

Sign-off

Deep-linkable — people will send each other a link to a section. The final section carries a recommendation that a human must write; it cannot be approved without one, and that is a regulatory requirement rather than a preference. Design it as a deliberate, weighty moment.

/deals/:dealId/activityActivity
Job

A complete, ordered record of what happened to this deal and who did it.

Contains

Documents added and superseded, extractions run, sections drafted and regenerated, comments, edits, the recommendation being written, approvals granted and withdrawn — each with a person and a timestamp.

States

Populated · filtered by kind or person

Note

This is an audit record under financial-services rules, so it reads as a register rather than a social feed. No avatars-and-verbs styling; plain, dense, and exportable.

Settings

Firm-level configuration. Two of these shape how the AI writes, which makes them more consequential than settings screens usually are.

/settings/profileHouse style
Job

Review and adjust the voice, structure and brand learned at onboarding — and learn again from newer papers.

Contains

Same content as the onboarding confirmation step, plus brand assets, the papers it was learned from, a re-learn action, and the ability to delete an uploaded paper.

States

Current · re-learning · never learned (using the general profile)

/settings/guardrailsGuardrails
Job

Let a firm set its own rules for how the AI works — the things it must always do, always surface, and always call by the right name.

Contains
  • Wording — "we say facility, not loan"
  • Structure — section order, what must always appear
  • Always do this — an analytical step to run on every deal
  • Always flag this — a threshold that must be surfaced
  • Outside our appetite — property types to be flagged, not silently ignored
  • Each rule editable, removable, and dated
States

None set · rules active · rule being edited

Two layers

Above a firm's own rules sits platform safety, which applies to everyone and cannot be switched off: no unsupported claims, no promotional language, no machine-made recommendation, nothing adverse said about a person beyond the evidence, no reasoning from protected attributes, no legal determinations, and no motive imputed to a professional. A firm's rules only ever add to that; they can never relax it. Show the two clearly separated — a firm should be able to see what protects them as well as what they have configured.

Timing

Rules apply at the moment a paper is written and never retroactively, so adding a rule today cannot invalidate a paper signed last month. Each paper records which rules were in force when it was generated.

Note

Deliberately not a free-text box. Each rule is one of a fixed set of kinds, chosen so a firm cannot accidentally instruct the system to hide a problem or to decide a recommendation. Design it as composing rules from parts, not as writing instructions. The framing throughout is "add analysis", never "suppress it".

/settings/teamTeamadmin
Job

Manage who is in the firm's account and what they may do.

Contains
  • Members, their role, and when they were last active
  • Invite by email — an invitation works regardless of the invitee's email domain
  • Pending access requests from people who signed up at the firm's domain
  • Change role, remove member
  • No seat count. Users are unlimited at every tier — invite the whole credit team, including committee members who only read and comment
  • Sign-in policy — require two-factor for password sign-ins, and a place for single sign-on when it arrives
States

Sole member · several members · requests waiting · invitation sent

Note

Three roles. An analyst runs deals and marks up papers. A credit officer also writes the recommendation and approves. An admin also manages people and billing. Only a credit officer can approve, and that is a regulatory line, not a convenience.

/settings/billingBillingadmin
Job

Show what has been used, what it cost, and keep the card current.

Contains
  • Current tier, papers used against papers included, and a way to change plan
  • Whether the free first paper is still available, payment method, invoice history
  • Usage — a list of the papers approved this month, not an abstract meter
Note

Three tiers banded on papers per month — 3, 8, or unlimited by quote. Users are unlimited at every tier, so there is no seat count to manage anywhere in the product. Going over the band never blocks a paper: extras are charged at the tier rate, and the arithmetic makes the upgrade obvious without a sales email.

States

First paper still free · first paper used · card valid · payment failed · no card yet · over the monthly band

Note

When payment fails, be direct about what stops and what does not: no new papers, no approvals — but everything already finished stays readable and exportable. Never hold a firm's completed work hostage.

/settings/accountYour account
Job

Personal settings, distinct from the firm's.

Contains

Name, email, password change, sign out of other sessions, and two-factor setup with recovery codes. Someone who signed in through Microsoft or Google already has their organisation's policy applied and should be told so rather than asked to set it up twice.

States

Default · saving · saved

Not in this phase

Deliberately excluded, so nothing here needs designing yet. Several will arrive soon after launch — worth knowing they exist so the structure can accommodate them, but they should not shape the first build.

  • Firm-branded subdomains
  • Two people editing the same section simultaneously
  • Notifications and email digests
  • Single sign-on for identity providers other than Microsoft and Google
  • Sending papers for external signature
  • Direct publishing into design tools
  • Free-text guardrails
  • Folders, archiving, portfolio views
  • Multiple email domains for one firm
